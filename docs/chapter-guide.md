*Final Year Project Documentation Guide* 

 _Chapter 1: Problem Identification_ 
State the objective of your work: what you intend to do and the problem you're setting out to solve.

 _Chapter 2: Literature Review_ 
Cover what has already been done in this area, what's lacking or weak about the existing solutions, and the technology you're using to address those gaps.

 _Chapter 3: Methodology_ 
Explain how you intend to solve the problem you've identified. Justify your methodology and the choices you've made. This part is very important. Include enough detail that someone else could take your work and reproduce the same results.

 _Chapter 4: Implementation_ 
Discuss what you actually did to arrive at your results.

 _Chapter 5: Results, Testing & Discussion_ 
Share your results and test outcomes. Document everything with screenshots and supporting evidence.




Chapters I write (this chat / Claude in general):

Chapter 1 — Problem Identification. I turn your SRS (from Claude Code) or your own explanation into a clear objectives/problem statement.
Chapter 2 — Literature Review. I can search the web for existing solutions/papers in your domain and help you critically compare them against your approach. This one needs your steering — you tell me the domain/technologies, I dig up what's out there.
Chapter 3 — Methodology (the write-up/justification part). Once you and Claude Code have nailed down what you built and how, I'm good at articulating and justifying the reasoning in academic prose — "why this approach, why not the alternatives."

Chapters Claude Code writes (in VS Code, grounded in your actual repo):

Chapter 3 — Methodology (the technical detail part). It can pull exact steps, algorithms, tools, and configurations straight from your code so the "reproducible by someone else" requirement is actually met, not guessed at.
Chapter 4 — Implementation. This is entirely its territory — it can describe your actual architecture, modules, and code decisions because it's reading the real thing.
Chapter 5 — Results, Testing & Discussion. It can run your tests, capture real outputs/logs, and draft results sections grounded in what actually happened. (It can't take GUI screenshots for you — you'll still need to grab those yourself.)
